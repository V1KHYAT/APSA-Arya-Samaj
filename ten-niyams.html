The Ten Principles (Niyams) of Arya Samaj

The core guiding principles of the Arya Samaj.
The foundation of the Arya Samaj is built upon ten core principles (Niyams) established by Maharshi Dayanand Saraswati. These principles provide a practical, logical framework for seeking truth, living ethically, and fostering universal brotherhood. Below is each principle alongside its philosophical commentary.
ओ३म्
1. Knowledge 2. God's Nature 3. The Vedas 4. Truth 5. Dharma 6. Welfare 7. Love & Justice 8. Vidya (Knowledge) 9. Shared Success 10. Social Duty
The Sacred Principle

The first (efficient) cause of all true knowledge and all that is known through knowledge is Eeshwar (the Supreme, i.e. GOD).

Philosophical Commentary

An efficient cause is the force that brings something into existence. Just as Eeshwar shapes timeless, formless Prakriti into the physical universe, He is the ultimate source of all reality. For the Arya Samaj, God is Truth, and true knowledge—whether realized through everyday awareness, deep yogic intuition, or the foundational insights of the ancient Rishis—stems directly from Him. Our faith, at its core, is a commitment to seeking and living by that Truth.

The Sacred Principle

Eeshwar (GOD) is existent, intelligent, and blissful. He is formless, omniscient, just, merciful, unborn, endless, unchangeable, beginningless, unequalled, the support of all, the master of all, omnipresent, immanent, unaging, immortal, fearless, eternal, and holy, and the maker of all. He alone is to be worthy of worship.

Philosophical Commentary

The Second Principle outlines the nature of Parameshvara (God) as Sat-Chit-Ananda—pure Existence, Consciousness, and Bliss. As a formless, infinite, and changeless Spirit, God is beyond birth, death, or physical limitation. Unbound by fear or hatred, He perfectly balances mercy and justice, providing everything souls need to live and reap the results of their actions. He is entirely self-sufficient, requiring no external help. Because God is formless (Nirakara) and purely equitable, Arya Samaj rejects flattery, idols, and the doctrine of divine incarnation—no human or creature can replace Him. Instead, true worship is reserved for God alone.

The Sacred Principle

The Vedas are the scriptures of all true knowledge. It is the paramount duty of Aryas to read them, teach them, recite them, and hear them being read.

Philosophical Commentary

The Vedas are the primary source of all true knowledge. It is our duty as Aryas to study, teach, recite, and listen to these sacred scriptures, using them as our guide for spiritual growth.

The Sacred Principle

One should always be ready to accept truth and renounce untruth.

Philosophical Commentary

We are committed to seeking the truth above all else. We remain open to new understanding and are always ready to renounce any belief that is proven false.

The Sacred Principle

All acts ought to be performed in conformity to Dharma i.e., after deliberating what is right and wrong.

Philosophical Commentary

Our actions must align with Dharma. Before acting, we weigh right against wrong, ensuring our behavior is guided by moral principles rather than impulse.

The Sacred Principle

The prime object of the Arya Samaj is to do good to the world, i.e., to ameliorate physical, spiritual and social welfare of all.

Philosophical Commentary

The mission of the Arya Samaj is to uplift the world. We work to improve the physical, spiritual, and social well-being of all, actively engaging in service that creates a positive impact.

The Sacred Principle

All ought to be treated with affection, Justice, righteousness and due regard to their merits.

Philosophical Commentary

We treat every individual with affection, justice, and respect. We recognize the inherent merit in others and act with compassion and fairness in all our dealings.

The Sacred Principle

One should promote Vidya (realization of true knowledge) and dispel Avidya (ignorance).

Philosophical Commentary

We actively promote Vidya (real knowledge) while working to dispel Avidya (ignorance). Our goal is to replace confusion with clarity, fostering an environment of learning and enlightenment.

The Sacred Principle

One should not be content with one’s own welfare alone, but should strive for one’s own welfare in the welfare of all.

Philosophical Commentary

We recognize that individual growth is inseparable from social progress. We strive for our own success by contributing to the welfare of others, understanding that our well-being is deeply connected to the community.

The Sacred Principle

In all matters affecting the well-being of the society the individual should subordinate his personal likings. In matters affecting him alone one should enjoy the freedom of action.

Philosophical Commentary

We balance personal freedom with social duty. While we enjoy individual liberty, we willingly prioritize the common good whenever the well-being of society depends on our commitment.

Living the Ten Niyams

These ten principles form the bedrock of daily spiritual discipline, community service, and ethical living for Aryas worldwide. Explore how APSA translates these principles into active education, relief, and national initiatives.
