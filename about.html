About APSA

A century of Vedic leadership — preserving, promoting, and practising the principles of Swami Dayanand Saraswati across North America.

Our Story

A Century of Vedic Leadership

The Arya Pratinidhi Sabha of America traces its roots to the early 20th century, when Arya Samaj immigrants from South Asia brought with them the reformist teachings of Swami Dayanand Saraswati — a vision of a society guided by reason, the Vedas, and universal brotherhood.

APSA was formally established to coordinate the growing number of Arya Samaj centres across the United States, creating a unified platform for spiritual education, community service, and the preservation of Vedic heritage in the diaspora.

Today, APSA serves as the apex body representing over 50 affiliated Arya Samaj organisations, conducting annual Sammelans, supporting youth programs, and providing resources to communities from coast to coast.

EARLY 1900s
First Arya Samaj Centres Established
Early South Asian immigrants establish the first Arya Samaj prayer halls in New York and New Jersey, conducting havan and Vedic study circles.
1930s
Formation of Regional Coordination
Growing Samaj communities in major cities begin formal coordination, hosting inter-Samaj events and joint Sammelans.
1970s
APSA Formally Incorporated
APSA is officially constituted as the representative body for Arya Samaj organisations across the United States and Canada.
1990s
Vedic Sanskriti Schools Launched
Recognising the need for youth education, APSA launches the VSS curriculum — now taught at 20+ locations nationwide.
2010s
Digital Expansion & Youth Wing
APSA launches VAYU — the Vedic Arya Youth Union — and begins digitalising the Vedic library, reaching thousands online.
Today
Serving 50+ Communities
APSA continues its mission with over 50 affiliated Samajs, 15 active programs, and a growing global diaspora network.

Foundational Principles

The Ten Niyams of Arya Samaj

Established by Swami Dayanand Saraswati, the Ten Niyams (rules) form the ideological foundation of every Arya Samaj worldwide. They are a comprehensive guide to individual conduct, community responsibility, and the pursuit of truth.

God is the efficient cause of all true knowledge and all that is known by means of it. This niyam establishes the supremacy of God as the ultimate source of wisdom and the foundation of all existence.
God is without form, omnipotent, just, merciful, unborn, infinite, unchangeable, without beginning, incomparable, the support of all, the master of all, omnipresent, omniscient and controller of all from within.
The Vedas are the scripture of all true knowledge. It is the paramount duty of all Aryas to read them, teach them, recite them, and hear them being recited. The Vedas are eternal revelations from God to humanity.
One should always be ready to accept truth and to renounce untruth. This pursuit of truth over tradition or convenience is central to the Arya Samaj reformist spirit.
All acts should be done in conformity with virtue, i.e. after due consideration of right and wrong. Individual conduct must be grounded in righteousness, not expediency.
The prime object of Arya Samaj is to do good to the world — that is, to promote physical, spiritual, and social good of all. This niyam anchors APSA's service mission.
All ought to be treated with love, justice, and with regard to their merit. Love for all creation — not just one's own community — is essential to Arya Samaj ethics.
One should dispel avidya (ignorance) and promote vidya (knowledge). Education — of the Vedas, of science, of ethics — is a sacred duty for every Arya.
One's own progress should be dependent on the upliftment of all others. Individual and collective progress are inseparable — a foundational principle of community service.
All persons should subordinate their personal benefit to social benefit — in matters of social, ethical, and regulatory conduct. This final niyam grounds all individual action in collective responsibility.

Governance

Constitution & Governance

APSA is governed by a democratically elected Executive Committee and operates in accordance with a formally adopted Constitution ratified by member Samajs. The organisation is a registered non-profit operating under 501(c)(3) status in the United States.

The Annual General Meeting (AGM) is held during the Maha Sammelan each year. All affiliated Samajs are entitled to send delegates, vote on resolutions, and participate in committee elections.
